# Bibliography

**Two sections. The separation is the point.**

Section A entries had their bibliographic details confirmed against primary or
authoritative secondary sources (ACL Anthology, publisher pages, DOI registries,
official EU sources) during preparation of these documents.

Section B entries are works believed to exist and to be relevant, whose exact
details — authors, year, venue, pages, DOI — have **not** been confirmed here.
**No Section B entry may appear in a submission until it is verified and moved to
Section A.** Several are near-certain classics; that is not a substitute for
checking.

No DOI, venue, page range, or publication detail in this file was reconstructed
from memory. Where a field was not confirmed, it is omitted rather than guessed.

## Section A — Verified

**[Aristotle2009]**
Aristotle. *The Nicomachean Ethics.* Translated by David Ross, revised by
Lesley Brown. Oxford University Press, 2009. Book VI distinguishes scientific
knowledge from practical wisdom and emphasizes practical judgment concerning
variable particulars. *Used as a conceptual lens, not as a claim that PERSONA
directly measures either virtue.*

**[Dey2001]**
Anind K. Dey. *Understanding and Using Context.* Personal and Ubiquitous
Computing, 5(1), 2001, pp. 4–7. doi:10.1007/s007790170019.
*Operational definition of context and the situation abstraction for
context-aware applications.*

**[Dourish2004]**
Paul Dourish. *What We Talk About When We Talk About Context.* Personal and
Ubiquitous Computing, 8(1), 2004, pp. 19–30.
doi:10.1007/s00779-003-0253-8. *Interactional critique of treating context as a
fixed representational container.*

**[Cheng2025HumT]**
Myra Cheng, Sunny Yu, Dan Jurafsky. *HumT DumT: Measuring and controlling
human-like language in LLMs.* Proceedings of the 63rd Annual Meeting of the
Association for Computational Linguistics (Volume 1: Long Papers), Vienna,
Austria, 2025, pp. 25983–26008. arXiv:2502.13259.
ACL Anthology: `2025.acl-long.1261`. Code: `github.com/myracheng/humtdumt`.
*Source of the HumT metric used for `H` in this project.*

**[Abercrombie2023]**
Gavin Abercrombie, Amanda Cercas Curry, Tanvi Dinkar, Verena Rieser, Zeerak
Talat. *Mirages. On Anthropomorphism in Dialogue Systems.* Proceedings of the
2023 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, Singapore,
2023, pp. 4776–4790. doi:10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.290. arXiv:2305.09800.

**[Epley2007]**
Nicholas Epley, Adam Waytz, John T. Cacioppo. *On Seeing Human: A Three-Factor
Theory of Anthropomorphism.* Psychological Review, 114(4), 2007, pp. 864–886.
doi:10.1037/0033-295X.114.4.864.

**[Waytz2010]**
Adam Waytz, John Cacioppo, Nicholas Epley. *Who Sees Human? The Stability and
Importance of Individual Differences in Anthropomorphism.* Perspectives on
Psychological Science, 5(3), 2010, pp. 219–232.

**[Epley2008]**
Nicholas Epley, Adam Waytz, Scott Akalis, John T. Cacioppo. *When We Need a
Human: Motivational Determinants of Anthropomorphism.* Social Cognition, 26(2),
2008, pp. 143–155. doi:10.1521/soco.2008.26.2.143.

**[Sharma2020]**
Ashish Sharma, Adam S. Miner, David C. Atkins, Tim Althoff. *A Computational
Approach to Understanding Empathy Expressed in Text-Based Mental Health Support.*
Proceedings of the 2020 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language
Processing (EMNLP), 2020, pp. 5263–5276.
doi:10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-main.425.

**[Leong2019]**
Brenda Leong, Evan Selinger. *Robot Eyes Wide Shut: Understanding Dishonest
Anthropomorphism.* Proceedings of the Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and
Transparency (FAT* '19), Atlanta, GA, January 29–31 2019, pp. 299–308.
doi:10.1145/3287560.3287591.

**[CounselBench2025]**
*CounselBench: A Large-Scale Expert Evaluation and Adversarial Benchmarking of
Large Language Models in Mental Health Question Answering.* arXiv:2506.08584.
Code and data: `github.com/llm-eval-mental-health/CounselBench`.
Contents: CounselBench-EVAL (2,000 expert evaluations by 100 mental health
professionals across six clinically grounded dimensions, on questions from
CounselChat) and CounselBench-Adv (120 expert-authored adversarial questions).
Reported Krippendorff's alpha ≥ 0.7 across dimensions.
*Source of the mental-health prompts used in this project.*
⚠ **Author list and final venue not captured — complete before citing.**

**[EUAIAct2024]**
Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (EU Artificial Intelligence Act), Article 50 —
*Transparency obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems.*
Article 50(1) requires that natural persons be informed they are interacting with
an AI system unless this is obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and
circumspect person. Transparency obligations enforceable from 2 August 2026, with
a transitional period to 2 December 2026 for the Article 50(2) marking
obligations. Official text: `ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50`.

**[DeVrio2025]**
Alicia DeVrio, Myra Cheng, Lisa Egede, Alexandra Olteanu, Su Lin Blodgett.

**[Cheng2025Dehumanizing]**
Myra Cheng, Su Lin Blodgett, Alicia DeVrio, Lisa Egede, Alexandra Olteanu.
*Dehumanizing Machines: Mitigating Anthropomorphic Behaviors in Text Generation

**[Kaminski2017]**
Margot Kaminski, Matthew Ruben, William D. Smart, Cindy M. Grimm. *Averting Robot
